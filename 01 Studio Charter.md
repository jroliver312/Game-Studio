# Studio Charter

Oct 15, 2026 · @Jack · Draft for the Owner to confirm

*What the studio is, why it exists, what it makes and refuses to make, how it works, and what success looks like. Every other studio document answers to this one. Sections marked **Proposed** are drafts; the Owner rewrites or confirms them and logs the result in the Studio Decision Log (08).*

## 1. What the studio is

**Name:** *not chosen yet* (Decision S-01). Until then, "the studio".

A one-person game studio with an AI assistant, run as a real business rather than a hobby. The Owner (Jack) designs, builds, ships and supports the games; the Assistant drafts, researches, automates and keeps the records; outside readers and testers check the work. It sells premium games on Steam first.

**Legal form today:** Jack as a sole proprietor. The game business is kept as its own entity in Olive's books, separate from the real estate business. Forming an LLC is a 2027 decision (S-02, S-11).

**Relationship to Jack's other work:**

| Olive | Olive runs the studio's business side (money, taxes, sales, marketing spend, keys register, feedback loop). The studio is Olive's first game-business customer (06). |

## 2. Purpose — Proposed

> Make deep, complete, fairly priced games that one person can build and support for years, and run the studio well enough that each game pays for the next.

Why it exists, in order:

1. **To make the games Jack wants to play and nobody ships** — systems-heavy simulations where the player's choices carry real weight.
2. **To be a working business**, not a folder of side projects: every game has a plan, a budget, a gate and a post-mortem.
3. **To prove the operating model** — that a solo developer with good process, good records and an AI assistant can run "the garage version of a AAA launch". The same model feeds Olive's design for small businesses.

## 3. What the studio makes — Proposed

- **Single-player, premium, complete at launch.** One price, the whole game. Updates fix and deepen; they do not sell back what was cut.
- **Systems and simulation first:** management, strategy, political and sports simulation, where a deterministic core and data-driven content let one person build depth.
- **PC on Steam first.** Other platforms only after a game has shipped on Steam and earned the port.
- **Original from the first word.** Fictional names, clubs, nations and people; no licensed or borrowed IP. "Free" data is used only after its licence is read (procedure rule 7).

## 4. What the studio will not make — Proposed

- Loot boxes, paid randomized rewards, energy timers, or anything designed to extract money rather than deliver play.
- Games that need servers the studio must run for the game to work.
- Games built on someone else's IP, real people or real organizations.
- A game the Owner cannot finish solo at measured hours — judged first by the idea card's size guess (03 §2), then by the production calendar. Bigger ideas are shelved until the studio is bigger.

Any exception is a Decision Log row with a reason, not a quiet drift.

## 6. Principles

These are the studio-wide versions of the procedure's rules (procedure §6), which still apply to every game.

1. **Foundations before features** — at the studio level as well as the game level: name, entity, accounts and records before the first store page.
2. **Typed numbers are targets; measured numbers are facts.** Calendars come from logged hours.
3. **One game in build at a time.** Ideas never interrupt a build; they go to the inbox.
4. **Gates are checklists, passed by the Owner.** The Assistant drafts and reminds, never passes a gate.
5. **Do it by hand once, write it down the second time, automate the third.** Automation never makes a decision or passes a gate.
6. **Nothing is finished until someone who did not write it has read it.**
7. **Every figure carries a date and a source.**
8. **The record is never deleted.** Reversals are new rows.
9. **Sustainable pace.** The studio shares Jack's week with a full-time job, Olive and the homestead. A plan that only works by borrowing from those is a bad plan.

## 7. What success looks like — Proposed

Success is measured at three levels. Numbers below are proposals for the Owner to set (S-13); blanks are left blank on purpose.

### 7.1 Per game

Each game's Strategy Tests document sets its own commercial tiers. The studio adds three floors every game must clear to count as a success regardless of tier:

- **Shipped complete**, on the date the production calendar set from measured hours (or a logged reason why not).
- **Reviews:** "Mostly Positive" or better (70%+) on Steam once it has 50+ user reviews.
- **Post-mortem written** within 30 days of launch, and the procedure updated from it.

### 7.2 Studio, money ladder

| Rung | Meaning | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 — Pays its own way | Lifetime game revenue covers every studio cost to date (Steam fees, tools, legal review, contract art) | Studio costs in Olive ≤ net revenue |
| 1 — Self-funding | A year's revenue covers the next game's cash ceiling | ______ per year |
| 2 — Meaningful income | Studio net income replaces a stated share of Jack's salary | ______ % |
| 3 — Optional job | Studio net income could replace the job | ______ per year, held for 12 months |

Honest framing: most Steam releases earn little, and a first commercial game is mainly a learning tier. Rung 0 on the first game is a good result; plans must not depend on rung 2.

### 7.3 Studio, operating health (reviewed quarterly)

- Hours from Stage 1 to Gate A falling game over game (procedure §8).
- **Zero** foundation decisions reversed after a game's first milestone.
- **Zero** licensing or data problems found after a build started.
- Weekly review held at least 3 weeks in 4.
- No Proposed decision older than its decide-by date.
- Average studio hours per week inside the capacity the Owner set (02 §5), with no month over it two months running.

### 7.4 Three-year picture — Proposed

By the end of 2029: two or more games shipped on Steam; the studio at rung 1; a back catalog that sells in seasonal sales without new work; each new game set up faster than the last; and the studio's process is good enough to be a template inside Olive for other small creative businesses.

## 8. Changing this charter

The charter changes only by a Decision Log row. It is re-read at every quarterly review and after every post-mortem. Sections are appended, never renumbered.
